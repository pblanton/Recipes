# Basic Biscuits

*A simple roll-and-cut biscuit made with everyday all-purpose flour and baked in a skillet.*

**Servings:** About 36 small biscuits | **Prep Time:** ~10 min | **Cook Time:** ~12 min | **Total Time:** ~25 min

## About This Recipe

A straightforward, no-frills biscuit — all-purpose flour instead of biscuit flour, a quick well-and-knead mixing method, and a short bake in a buttered skillet rather than on a sheet pan. Denser and more rustic than a fold-and-roll biscuit, and quick to pull together.

## Ingredients

- 2 cups all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon sugar
- 1 tablespoon baking powder
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 8 tablespoons butter or beef tallow, cubed
- 3/4 cup milk, more or less as needed

## Directions

1. Preheat the oven to 425°F.
2. In a large bowl, combine the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt. Cut in the butter (or tallow) until the mixture looks like coarse cornmeal.
3. Make a well in the center of the flour mixture and slowly add the milk. Knead the dough gently with your fingers, adding a bit more milk if it seems dry.
4. Roll the dough out on a lightly floured surface to your desired thickness, then cut with a small biscuit cutter.
5. Butter the bottom of a skillet, arrange the biscuits in the pan, and bake for 12 minutes, or until golden brown.

## Notes & Tips

- Beef tallow is the preferred fat for this binder in place of lard or vegetable shortening — keep it cold and cubed going into the flour, same as butter.
- Cut smaller than you'd expect for a full-size biscuit; this recipe yields about 36, so the cutter size is doing a lot of the work on final texture.
