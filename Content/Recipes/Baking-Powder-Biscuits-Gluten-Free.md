# Baking Powder Biscuits (Gluten-Free)

*A gluten-free adaptation of the classic drop-or-cut biscuit, built on a 1:1 gluten-free flour blend.*

**Servings:** Not specified in original | **Prep Time:** ~10 min | **Cook Time:** 15–20 min | **Total Time:** ~30 min

## About This Recipe

Same technique as the original biscuit dough, swapped onto a gluten-free flour blend. Gluten-free flours absorb liquid differently and give you none of wheat's structural gluten, so the dough is a little wetter and benefits from resting briefly before baking and from not being reworked or re-rolled more than once.

## Ingredients

- 3 cups gluten-free 1:1 baking flour blend (one that includes xanthan or guar gum, such as Bob's Red Mill 1:1 or King Arthur Measure for Measure)
- 1/2 teaspoon xanthan gum (omit if your blend already contains it)
- 1 teaspoon salt
- 1 tablespoon baking powder
- 1/2 teaspoon baking soda (optional — only if using buttermilk or acidified milk, see Notes)
- 1 to 4 tablespoons sugar, to taste
- 4 to 6 tablespoons butter or beef tallow, cold or well frozen
- 1 egg (optional — helps bind the dough, see Notes)
- 1 to 1 1/4 cups buttermilk, milk, or water (start with 1 cup and add more as needed)

## Directions

1. Preheat the oven to 425°F.
2. Whisk the dry ingredients together (including the baking soda, if using). Cut or rub the cold butter (or tallow) into the dry mix with two knives, a pastry blender, or your fingertips until it resembles coarse breadcrumbs. **Alternative method:** freeze the butter or tallow solid and grate it directly into the dry mix instead — this keeps distinct chunks of fat intact through mixing, which can help build flakier layers, especially useful since gluten-free dough doesn't have gluten strands to do that work for you.
3. If using the egg, whisk it into the buttermilk first. Add the liquid all at once, starting with 1 cup, and mix quickly and gently until a soft, slightly tacky dough forms. Gluten-free flours vary in how much liquid they soak up — add the extra liquid a tablespoon at a time only if the dough seems dry or crumbly rather than soft.
4. Let the dough rest for 5–10 minutes before shaping; this gives the gum and starches time to hydrate and firms the dough up slightly, making it easier to handle.
5. **For drop biscuits:** Drop the dough by the spoonful onto a lightly floured (use gluten-free flour) baking sheet, or fill the cups of a greased muffin tin about two-thirds full.
6. **For cut biscuits:** Pat the dough into a rectangle about 3/4 inch thick directly on parchment paper (gluten-free dough is stickier and harder to transfer once rolled). Cut into rounds with a biscuit cutter, or into squares or diamonds with a bench knife, dipping the cutter in gluten-free flour between cuts.
7. Bake for 15 to 20 minutes, until lightly browned. Remove from the oven and serve warm.

## Notes & Tips

- Beef tallow is the preferred fat for this binder — refined/deodorized tallow gives the closest flavor and texture to lard while keeping things clean. Keep it cold going into the flour, same as butter.
- Skip the letter-fold-and-reroll step used in the wheat version — gluten-free dough doesn't build flaky layers the same way, and reworking it toughens the texture instead of improving it.
- If your flour blend already contains xanthan or guar gum (most off-the-shelf 1:1 blends do), leave out the extra 1/2 teaspoon xanthan gum.
- **Grated frozen fat:** an alternative to cutting in cold butter/tallow — freeze it solid and grate it into the dry mix instead. Keeps fat chunks distinct through mixing, which tends to help flakiness, and is worth trying if the cut-in method isn't giving you the texture you want.
- **Baking soda + acid:** if you use buttermilk (or milk with a squeeze of lemon juice or 1/4 teaspoon vinegar) as the liquid, add the optional 1/2 teaspoon baking soda. The acid-soda reaction gives extra lift and a slight tang. If using plain milk or water, skip the baking soda.
- **Egg:** gluten-free dough can be crumbly since it has no gluten to hold it together. If yours comes out that way, whisk in the optional egg with the liquid — it helps bind the dough. Not necessary if your dough is already coming together well without it.
- These biscuits are best the day they're baked; gluten-free baked goods tend to dry out faster than wheat-based ones. Wrap leftovers tightly and warm before serving.
