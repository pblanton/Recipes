# Numbing Sichuan-Style Hot Pot

*A fiery, tongue-numbing communal hot pot built around the classic Sichuan "mala" combo of chiles and Sichuan peppercorns.*

**Servings:** 4–6 | **Prep Time:** 2 hrs 10 min | **Cook Time:** 1 hr 15 min | **Total Time:** ~3 hrs 25 min

## About This Recipe

Hot pot is a shared, simmer-at-the-table meal built around a broth loaded with dried chiles and Sichuan peppercorns, which together create the signature "mala" sensation — a burning heat cooled by a buzzing numbness. Diners cook their own raw meats, seafood, and vegetables directly in the pot and dip them in a simple sesame-garlic sauce. The base can be made a day ahead and reheated, and the ingredient spread is meant to be varied — a little of a lot, rather than a lot of a little.

## Ingredients

**Hot Pot Base**
- 2 cups dried Sichuan chile peppers (such as facing heaven or er jing tiao), divided
- 1/4 cup whole green Sichuan peppercorns, divided
- 1/4 cup whole red Sichuan peppercorns
- 2 black cardamom pods
- 2 cinnamon sticks
- 6 dried whole bay leaves
- 3 star anise
- 1 tablespoon cumin seeds
- 1 tablespoon fennel seeds
- 6 whole cloves
- 1/2 cup Shaoxing cooking wine
- 1 cup avocado oil
- 1 cup beef tallow (or substitute with more avocado oil)
- 10 scallions, roots trimmed (6 finely chopped, 4 cut into 3-inch segments)
- 1 (4-inch) piece ginger, peeled and roughly chopped
- 7 garlic cloves, roughly chopped
- 1 stalk lemongrass, tough outer layers removed, cut into 1-inch chunks
- 1/2 cup doubanjiang (chile bean paste)
- 1/4 cup douchi (fermented black beans)
- 1/2 cup Sichuan chile flakes
- 1 teaspoon rock sugar
- 5 cups beef or chicken stock, plus more for topping off
- 1 cube mushroom or chicken bouillon
- Kosher salt, to taste

**Dunking Ingredients** (pick 2–3 from each group)
- Raw thin-sliced beef or lamb
- Sliced fish fillet (tilapia, cod, or halibut) or fish balls made from clean fish
- Organ meats from clean animals (e.g., chicken gizzards, beef tripe)
- Whole bok choy, crown daisy leaves, winter melon (1-inch chunks), Napa cabbage (2-inch pieces), or seaweed knots
- Thinly sliced daikon, turnip, lotus root, or potato
- Shiitake or portobello mushrooms (bite-size chunks), or whole enoki mushrooms
- Cooked, peeled whole quail eggs
- Fried tofu puffs
- Non-starchy noodles (e.g., sweet potato starch noodles)

**Dipping Sauce**
- Toasted sesame oil
- Finely grated garlic
- Chopped fresh scallions
- Chopped fresh cilantro
- Soy sauce
- Black vinegar

## Directions

1. **Prep the aromatics.** Cover 1½ cups of the dried chiles with water in a small pot, boil for 20 minutes, then drain and chop or pulse into a rough paste. Set aside 2 tablespoons of the whole green peppercorns. Coarsely grind the rest of the green peppercorns, the red peppercorns, cardamom, cinnamon, bay leaves, star anise, cumin, fennel, and cloves. Cover the ground spices with the Shaoxing wine and let steep 30 minutes.
2. **Build the base.** Heat the avocado oil (and beef tallow, if using) in a wide pot over medium heat. Cook the finely chopped scallions, ginger, garlic, and lemongrass until fragrant, about 2 minutes. Stir in the chopped chiles, doubanjiang, douchi, and chile flakes, and let cook gently on low for 15 minutes.
3. **Simmer the broth.** Add the steeped ground spices (with their liquid) and the rock sugar; simmer on low for 10–15 minutes to reduce slightly. Pour in the stock, bring to a boil, then reduce to low and simmer 10 more minutes. Turn off the heat and let the broth steep at least 30 minutes while you prep the dunking ingredients. (The base can be cooled and refrigerated up to a day ahead.)
4. **Serve.** Strain out the solids with a slotted spoon. Stir in the reserved peppercorns and dried chiles, the scallion segments, bouillon cube, and salt to taste. Bring the pot to a boil on a portable burner at the table, then reduce to a gentle simmer. Mix dipping sauces (start with a 1:1 sesame oil to garlic ratio) while the broth heats. Dunk ingredients until cooked through — meat and fish cook fast, so watch them closely — and top off with more stock as the level drops.

## Notes & Tips

- Aim for 3–4 pieces of each meat/fish and 5–6 pieces of each vegetable/other item per diner.
- Beef tallow adds richness but is optional; avocado oil alone works fine.
- Leftover base freezes well for a future hot pot night.
